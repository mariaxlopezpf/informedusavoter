Help me decide · research tools and a 15-minute plan
  1. Pick your top 3 issues, such as property insurance, housing costs, taxes, schools, flooding, or the economy.
  2. Look up your exact ballot by address so you only research races you'll actually see.
  3. Compare candidates' own answers on Vote411 or the public-media guide below. Every candidate gets the same questions.
  4. Read each race's "Where they stand" links. They were picked to cover the candidates on both sides.
  5. Check claims you hear in ads with a fact-checker. For amendments, look at what Yes and No each mean and who's funding each side.
  6. It's OK to skip races. Leaving a race blank doesn't spoil the rest of your ballot.
